Morgan Spurlock (1970-2024)

The director of “Super Size Me”, who ate exclusively at McDonald's for thirty days in front of a camera — and sparked a debate that continues to this day.

In brief

  • Born in 1970, died in 2024.
  • Super Size Me (2004): thirty days of eating only McDonald's food, under filmed medical supervision. Nominated for the Oscar for Best Documentary.
  • The film is a first-person account, not a scientific study: its protocol has no clinical value.
  • McDonald's dropped the “Super Size” option in the United States in 2004, denying any link with the film.

Super Size Me brought the nutrition issue into public debate at the very moment when the American courts, in the Pelman case, were declining to rule on a chain's liability for its customers' obesity.

Its significance is that of a media object, not of evidence: one man, thirty days, no control group. It nonetheless durably associated the brand with a public health issue — and made visible, by contrast, the figure of Don Gorske, who appeared in the film, and who has eaten a Big Mac a day since the 1970s with no measurable effect on his health.
