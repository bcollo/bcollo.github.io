Major lawsuits · 12 December 2026

Slivered onions, 104 people ill and one dead: the E. coli outbreak that hit the Quarter Pounder

In autumn 2024, an E. coli O157:H7 outbreak linked to McDonald's Quarter Pounders affected fourteen US states. An onion recall, the burger's withdrawal, a series of lawsuits and a 100-million-dollar plan: a look back at a health crisis and its legal aftermath.

This article is a background document. It reports public facts and court decisions, without taking sides. The sources are cited at the end of the article; reported statements are attributed to their authors.

On 22 October 2024, the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) opens an investigation into an outbreak of Escherichia coli O157:H7. Most of the patients have one thing in common, they had all ate a Quarter Pounder, McDonald's burger with a quarter-pound patty. On 3 December 2024, the investigation is closed, the official toll is 104 cases, 34 hospitalisations and one death, in fourteen states.

An official toll and a lead: the onions

First of all, we have to look at the authorities' figures. According to the FDA, the US food agency, the patients lived mainly in the West and the centre of the country, from Colorado to Wyoming. The first illnesses date from 12 September and the last from 21 October 2024. Four people developed haemolytic uraemic syndrome, a complication that can lead to kidney failure. The person who died was an elderly person from Colorado, who wasnt one of these four cases. According to the CDC, the real number of people who fell ill is probably much higher.

Then, the investigators looked for the ingredient responsible. At the beginning, two leads was there: the patty and the slivered onions. The Food Safety and Inspection Service of the Department of Agriculture (FSIS) ruled out the ground beef. The onion lead, for it's part, got stronger. Of 81 people interviewed, 80 had eaten at McDonald's, and 63 of the 75 people who remembered their order had had a product with fresh slivered onions.

“The E. coli strain linked to illnesses in this outbreak was not found in onion samples or in the environment; however, epidemiological and traceback evidence show that the recalled yellow onions were the likely source of this outbreak.”FDA, 3 December 2024 - translated from English

The conclusion therefore rests on the investigations, not on a positive test of the onions. The onions supplier was the company Taylor Farms. According to the FDA, the onions in question came from a Taylor Farms facility in Colorado. On 22 October 2024, Taylor Farms launched a voluntary recall of these yellow onions, sold to McDonald's but also to other foodservice customers.

A burger withdrawn and then brought back

What is more, McDonald's had took action quickly. According to CNN and Restaurant Dive, the Quarter Pounder was withdrawn in about one fifth of its restaurants in the United States. Restaurants in Colorado, Kansas and Wyoming, and some of those in nine other states, stopped using slivered onions. Then, in mid-November 2024, the FDA states that McDonald's has reintroduced onions from another supplier across its whole menu. According to CNBC, the burger with its onions was back in all the restaurants in the country that same week.

Joe Erlinger, president of McDonald's USA, apologised to the customers affected: “we are sorry”.

Franchisees and the 100-million-dollar plan

The restaurant owners, most of them franchisees, also suffered from the crisis. According to CNBC, the chief financial officer Ian Borden told investors that sales and daily customer traffic was turning negative just after the CDC announcement.

In November 2024, McDonald's announced a plan of more than 100 million dollars. According to an internal memo seen by CNBC, 65 million are intended for restaurant owners who lost revenue, targeting the most affected states, and about 35 million go to programmes to bring customers back, including marketing.

A series of lawsuits

Suddenly, the lawyers was entering the scene. On 23 October 2024, the day after the CDC alert, Eric Stelly, a Colorado resident, sued McDonald's before the Circuit Court of Cook County, in Illinois, where the group has it's head office. His lawyers, Ron Simon & Associates and Meyers & Flowers, rely on product liability, negligence and breach of implied warranties, and claim 50,000 dollars per count, according to Restaurant Dive. The same lawyer then filed a complaint for Clarissa DeBock, a Nebraska resident.

The law firm Marler Clark, which specialises in food safety, also filed a complaint on behalf of Sarah Johnson before the federal District Court for the District of Nebraska, under number 4:24-cv-03195. These complaints contain allegations that have not been adjudicated.

To date, no public decision have been found in these cases.

The memory of Jack in the Box

This case brings to mind the outbreak that occured with Jack in the Box restaurants in 1992-1993. According to the CDC report published in April 1993, more than 500 confirmed infections and four deaths were recorded in the State of Washington, Idaho, California and Nevada, after people ate hamburgers from the same chain. The CDC considered that undercooking of the patties had “probably played an important role”. Bill Marler, of Marler Clark, presents him self as a food safety advocate since that case. The difference, its that in 2024 the beef was ruled out and it is a raw vegetable, added after cooking, that is named as the likely source.

To conclude, this crisis shows how a brand that closely controls its supply chain, its suppliers and its recipes, also carry the consequences of a problem at a single supplier before the courts and the public, while the franchisees suffer the fall in sales in their restaurants.

This article documents ongoing proceedings. The complaints mentioned contain allegations that have not been decided by a court, and McDonald's and Taylor Farms alike are entitled to the presumption that applies to any party that has not been found liable. The outcome of these actions is not known at the date of publication.
