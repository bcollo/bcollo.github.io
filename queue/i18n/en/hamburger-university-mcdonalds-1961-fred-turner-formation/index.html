Hamburger University: how McDonald's turned a basement in Illinois into the school of its worldwide network

Founded in 1961 by Fred Turner beneath a restaurant in Elk Grove Village, Hamburger University has been training McDonald's managers and franchisees for more than sixty years. A look back at the history of a training centre that became one of the tools the chain uses to enforce its standards.

This article is a background document. It reports public facts and court decisions, without taking sides. The sources are cited at the end of the article; reported statements are attributed to their authors.

In 1961, in the basement of a McDonald's restaurant in Elk Grove Village, Illinois, a small class takes lessons on how to run a restaurant. According to the companys history, the first class has 15 graduates, in February of that year. It is the beginning of Hamburger University, the training centre that McDonald's today presents as the heart of its internal learning.

Fred Turner, from the counter to the manual

First of all, the story of the school are linked to that of Fred Turner. In 1956, this 23-year-old young man answers an advertisement to open a franchise, then he works behind the counter of Ray Kroc's restaurant in Des Plaines. According to the Los Angeles Times, he is soon hired by the company, were he deals with operations and training, and he becomes the protégé of Kroc, who will buy the business from the McDonald brothers in 1961.

In 1958, Turner writes McDonald's first operations and training manual. The newspaper explains that this manual gave realy precise instructions: fries no thicker than 0.28 inch, no more than six patties on the grill at the same time. A 2011 book quoted by the daily paper sums up his approach like this:

“He tried to move restaurant management from the status of an art to that of a science.”Essentials of Strategic Management, 2011, quoted by the Los Angeles Times, 2013 - translated from English

This sentence clearly shows the original idea: Hamburger University is not a school in the traditional sense, it is used to pass on one identical method too everyone. What is more, according to Bloomberg, Turner was one of the architects of the so-called QSC doctrine, for quality, service and cleanliness. He would become CEO of McDonald's from 1974 to 1987, then chairman of the board untill 1990, he dies in January 2013 aged 80.

The “Bachelor of Hamburgerology” and the Oak Brook campus

Graduates recieve a “Bachelor of Hamburgerology”, according to the McDonald's website quoted by Bloomberg. The name makes people smile but the programme is suppose to be serious: according to McDonald's, it includes a course for restaurant managers, another for middle managers and programmes for executives.

Then, the school growed with the company. According to McDonald's, a worldwide Hamburger University campus opens in 1983 in Oak Brook, the Chicago suburb where the company had its head office, for 40 million dollars. In a 2011 press release, the company describes a 130,000-square-foot building on an 80-acre campus, with a 300-seat auditorium, laboratory kitchens, 64 full-time teachers and the ability to teach in 28 languages. In 2004, the Oak Brook centre is renamed after Fred L. Turner.

The return to Chicago and the campuses abroad

Then in June 2018, McDonald's leaves Oak Brook for a new 250-million-dollar head office in the West Loop district of Chicago, after 47 years in the suburbs according to CNBC. Hamburger University moves to the buildings second floor. Dezeen magazine relates that new recruits enters from Randolph Street, in a setting that imitates a university campus, with portraits of graduating classes like in a school yearbook. In 2019, the former Oak Brook site, including the training building, is sold to the entrepreneur John Paul DeJoria, the price was not made public according to the Daily Herald.

The school have also gone abroad. According to McDonald's, campuses opened in Tokyo (1971), London (1981), Munich (1982), Sydney (1989), São Paulo (1996) and Shanghai (2010). The AP news agency reports that at it's inauguration in March 2010, the Shanghai campus was presented as the seventh in the world, costing 250 million dollars, and that it replaced Hong Kongs school. Its dean, Susanna Li, said at the time:

“We will do our best to be the Harvard of our industry.”Susanna Li, dean of Hamburger University in Shanghai, 2010 - translated from English

Claimed figures and college credits

The number of graduates varies depending on the source and on who is counted. In 2005, McDonald's announced more than 75,000 graduates and about 5,000 students a year. In 2011, the company claimed more than 275,000 “franchisees, employees and suppliers” trained around the world. For its part, CNBC spoke in 2018 of more than 80,000 restaurant managers, middle managers and franchisees, so these figures do not measure the same thing.

As for academic recognition, McDonald's announced in November 2005 that the American Council on Education (ACE) had granted 46 college credit recommendations to its manager training, an increase of 77% according to the company, which presented itself as the only restaurant organisation in this position. In the same press release, an ACE official, Jo Ann Robinson, described the programme as a good example of training adapted to adults. In 2011, McDonald's mentioned 50 recommendations, and about 21 credit hours for an average manager, the equivalent of one semester.

A school serving the network's standards

To conclude, Hamburger University plays a role in the uniformity of the network. McDonald's itself writes that the school is used to establish consistent restaurant standards around the world. The AP explained in 2010 that a company rule requires that restaurants is run by managers trained at Hamburger University, and the 2011 press release names franchisees among the groups trained.

This story illustrates a central question in the relationship between a franchisor and its franchisees: the head of the network passes on know-how, and at the same time it sets the way it must be applied, from the thickness of the fries to the management of a team. Training thus becomes one of the means by which a brand keeps control of a network made up largely of independent businesses.
