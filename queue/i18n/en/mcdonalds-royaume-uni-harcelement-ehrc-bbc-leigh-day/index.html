McDonald's in the United Kingdom: an agreement against harassment, then hundreds of testimonies

In February 2023, McDonald's UK signed a legal agreement with the EHRC to protect its staff from sexual harassment. Five months later, more than 100 employees gave testimony to the BBC. Parliamentary hearings, a strengthened agreement and claims by more than 700 young employees followed.

This article is a background document. It reports public facts and court decisions, without taking sides. The sources are cited at the end of the article; reported statements are attributed to their authors.

On 8 February 2023, McDonald's Restaurants Limited signed a legal agreement with the Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC), the British equality regulator. This text, called a “section 23” agreement under the Equality Act 2006, folowed concerns about the way complaints of sexual harassment made by restaurant staff in the United Kingdom were being handled.

McDonald's committed to communicate a zero tolerence policy, to carry out an anonymous survey of employees, to strengthen its procedures and its training, and to help franchisees adopt these tools. At the time, Alistair Macrow, CEO of McDonald's for the UK and Ireland, said that the company allready had “a strong track record in this area”.

The BBC investigation of July 2023

The BBC started investigating precisely after this agreement was signed. For five months, there journalists contacted employees, then on 18 July 2023 it published their testimonies. More than 100 current or recent employees was describing a culture of sexual assault, harassment, racism and bullying.

According to the BBC, of these allegations, 31 concerned sexual assault and 78 sexual harassment, it also counted 18 allegations of racism and six of homophobia. Some employees were 17 years old. It should be remembered that the chain then employs more than 170,000 people and that three quarters of its employees are between 16 and 25, for many of them it is their first job. What is more, most of them are not employed directly by McDonald's but by franchisees, a question that has also been debated in the United States under the name of joint employer.

The company reacted quickly. Alistair Macrow admitted failings:

“There are clearly cases where we have fallen short, and for that we offer our deepest apologies.”Alistair Macrow, to the BBC, July 2023 - translated from English

On the EHRCs side, it said it was “concerned” and opened a dedicated email address to collect reports.

The head of McDonald's UK before MPs

On 14 November 2023, Alistair Macrow is summoned by the House of Commons Business and Trade Committee, in an inquiry entitled “Employment at McDonald's Franchises”. He first offered an apology “without reservation” too all the people affected. Then he gave figures: 407 reports received, 157 investigations completed, 75 disciplinary sanctions and 18 dismissals. He also stated that the franchisees, 193 in number, operate 89% of the business in the United Kingdom, a figure that the website Restaurant Online was still repeating in October 2024.

Then on 7 January 2025, the BBC published new testimonies, this time concerning events later then November 2023. The broadcaster said it had received more than 160 new accounts since its first investigation, and the EHRC 300 reports. Employees also claimed that the audits entrusted to the firm PwC had been “staged” by some restaurants, which PwC and McDonald's disputed by pointing to their procedures.

The same day, Alistair Macrow is once again before the committee. Its chair, Liam Byrne, asked him whether McDonald's had become “a predators' paradise”. The CEO replied that 75 allegations of sexual harassment had of been made in twelve months, that 47 had led to sanctions and that 29 people had been dismissed. He disputed the scale of the problem:

“The kinds of allegations you are describing are not widespread. They are isolated incidents that must be eliminated from our business.”Alistair Macrow, Business and Trade Committee, 7 January 2025 - translated from English

A regulator that strengthens its agreement

In March 2025, the EHRC wrote to the some 1,400 restaurants in England, Scotland and Wales. As the BBC pointed out, the franchisees werent covered by the 2023 agreement, so the regulator reminded them of there legal obligations and of the risk of enforcement action. McDonald's then said it was “confident” in its action plan.

Finally, on 7 November 2025, the EHRC announced the extension of the agreement, with a strengthened plan “in response to serious allegations”. McDonald's committed in particular to having its complaints handling unit audited by an outside body, to having complaints against managers investigated outside the restaurant, and to extending the training of managers, franchisees included, to social media and to the grooming of young employees. The company said it welcomed these measures.

More than 700 young employees before the tribunal

At the same time, the law firm Leigh Day launched a group action against McDonald's as early as November 2023. In January 2025, it said it represented more than 700 clients aged 19 or under at the time of the events. According to the firm, these people describe discrimination and harassment, these are allegations that havent been adjudicated. McDonald's, for its part, states that any incident of harassment is “unacceptable” and is promptly investigated.

On 9 January 2026, Leigh Day stated that these claims, filed before the Employment Tribunal and concerning more than 400 restaurants, were still waiting to be examined.

This case brings to light a question that often comes up in franchising: the employees are employed by independent franchisees, whereas the commitments made before the regulator are signed by the networks head. It is this link between the two that the EHRC has tried to clarify over the course of its interventions.

This article documents ongoing proceedings. The allegations made by the employees and their lawyers have not been adjudicated by the Employment Tribunal, and no decision had been delivered at the time of writing. McDonald's states that it has put an action plan in place and that it handles every report.
