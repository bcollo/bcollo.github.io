History · 6 November 2026

After more than thirty years in Russia, McDonald's sold some 850 restaurants to a Siberian licensee

Opened on Pushkin Square in January 1990, McDonald's Russian network, 84% owned by the parent company, closed in March 2022 before being sold to Alexander Govor, who operated 25 restaurants in Siberia. It reopened on 12 June under the brand “Vkusno i tochka”.

This article is a background document. It reports public facts and court decisions, without taking sides. The sources are cited at the end of the article; reported statements are attributed to their authors.

On 31 January 1990, a long queue formed around Pushkin Square, in Moscow, for the opening of the first McDonald's in the Soviet Union. According to George Cohon's obituary published by the New York Times, some 30,000 people tried the menu on the very first day; in 2010 the Moscow Times described this figure as a record for an opening day of the chain.

The restaurant was opened by McDonald's Canada, then headed by George Cohon. Thirty-two years later, it was at the same spot that the network would reopen under another name.

A “temporary” closure in March 2022

On 8 March 2022, McDonald's announced the temporary closure of its 850 restaurants in Russia, in response to the invasion of Ukraine, the Associated Press reported. The group stated that it would continue to pay its 62,000 employees in the country. In a letter to staff, its chief executive, Chris Kempczinski, wrote that the company's values prevented it from “ignoring the needless human suffering” in Ukraine.

According to the quarterly report filed with the SEC, the US securities regulator, the closure took effect in mid-March; as at 31 March, all the restaurants owned by the group in Russia and Ukraine were closed.

The structure of the Russian network directly exposed the parent company. According to the AP, unlike KFC, Pizza Hut or Burger King, whose Russian restaurants belong to franchisees, McDonald's owned 84% of its outlets in the country, a figure also given by Reuters. Worldwide, the company stated over the same period that around 95% of its restaurants were owned and operated by independent entrepreneurs.

16 May: the announcement of the exit

On 16 May 2022, McDonald's announced that it was leaving the Russian market “after more than 30 years of operations” and was initiating the sale of its entire portfolio to a local buyer. The press release cited the humanitarian crisis caused by the war in Ukraine and an operating environment that had become unpredictable, which in its view made continuing the business in Russia “untenable” and contrary to the group's values.

The company announced the “de-Arching” of the restaurants: they would stop using the McDonald's name, logo, visual identity and menu, while the group retained its trademarks in Russia. It said it wanted the employees to be paid until a transaction was concluded and to keep a job with the buyer.

“Our commitment to our values means that we can no longer make the Arches shine there.”Chris Kempczinski, president and chief executive officer of McDonald's, 16 May 2022 - translated from English

The group then anticipated a charge, mainly non-cash, of 1.2 to 1.4 billion dollars, to write down its net investment in the country and to recognise foreign exchange losses previously recorded in its equity.

A Siberian licensee takes over the network

On 19 May, McDonald's announced that it had found a buyer: Alexander Govor, one of its local licensees. According to Reuters, the AP and Interfax, he had been a franchisee of the chain since 2015 and operated 25 restaurants in Siberia through his company GiD. The AP and the Moscow Times also describe him as a co-owner of NefteKhimServis, a company in the oil sector.

The price was not disclosed. According to Reuters and the AP, the buyer undertook to keep the employees for at least two years on equivalent terms and to pay, until closing, the salaries of head-office staff spread across 45 regions. He also took over, according to CNBC and the Financial Times, the existing commitments to suppliers and landlords. McDonald's, according to the AP, intended to enforce its trademarks if necessary.

In early June, the Russian Federal Antimonopoly Service approved the transaction. In a statement of 2 June, it said, according to Reuters, that McDonald's would have an option to buy back the restaurants within fifteen years; Interfax, citing the same body, specified that this buyback would take place on market terms. Reuters noted that many of the terms of the sale remained unclear.

12 June: “Vkusno i tochka”

On 12 June 2022, Russia's national day, fifteen restaurants reopened in Moscow and the surrounding region, including the one on Pushkin Square, under the brand “Vkusno i tochka”, which the AP translates as “Tasty, full stop” and Reuters as “It's good, and that's all”. The new logo, according to the AP, “still evokes the golden arches”. The chain's chief executive, Oleg Paroev, was then aiming for around 200 restaurants open by the end of the month.

In its accounts, McDonald's states that it completed the sale in mid-June, marking “a complete exit from the market”. The group says it paid employees and rents until the agreement was signed. The charge ultimately recognised amounted to 1,281 million dollars before tax over the first nine months of 2022, mainly the write-down of the net investment in Russia and the cumulative foreign exchange losses.

The case of the franchisees

Restaurants run by franchisees did not follow the same timetable. The AP notes that some remained open during the suspension. In June, Oleg Paroev said that the other franchisees would have the option of operating under the new brand. According to Reuters, a source close to Rosinter, a franchisee present in railway stations and airports in Moscow and St Petersburg, said that these outlets would remain open until 2023. At the end of 2022, Rosinter announced that they were switching to the Vkusno i tochka brand.

The episode sheds light on a particular feature: while the vast majority of its restaurants worldwide are run by independent franchisees, in Russia the group owned most of its outlets. According to the AP, this structure gave it more control over operations than many of its competitors; the exit took place through the sale of the network to one of its own licensees, with McDonald's retaining ownership of its trademarks.
